# Product Design Ideation

Use when shaping a new product experience or making a substantial change to an existing flow. For a small, clearly specified UI edit, implement the request directly.

## Frame the problem

- Name the user, the task they need to complete, and the outcome that would count as success.
- Gather relevant product constraints, existing patterns, support evidence, and user-provided research.
- Separate observed facts from assumptions. Do not invent research or imply a concept has been validated.
- Ask only for missing decisions that would materially change the direction; otherwise state assumptions and proceed.

## Generate directions

- Develop two or three meaningfully different approaches when the problem supports alternatives.
- For each, explain the main interaction, benefits, costs, and risks in concrete terms.
- Keep options grounded in the user task, accessibility, platform constraints, and the existing product.
- Recommend one direction and explain which evidence or constraint drives the recommendation.

## Make it reviewable

Present a concise flow, outline, wireframe, or prototype when it helps compare options. State unresolved questions and what research or usability test could answer them. Do not describe a mockup as user-tested or production-ready without evidence.
