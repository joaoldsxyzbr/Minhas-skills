# Everyday Planning

Apply the [shared workflow rules](../using-minhas-skills/references/workflow-rules.md) once.

## Frame the practical decision

Identify the outcome and the constraints the user actually supplied: dates, location, budget, party size, availability, dietary needs or personal preferences when relevant. Ask only for an essential missing constraint that materially changes the recommendation. For small routine questions, answer directly rather than forcing a planning workflow.

Offer a practical comparison or sequence of actions appropriate to the task. Distinguish must-have constraints from preferences and call out consequential tradeoffs. When prices, schedules, inventory, regulations or business availability might have changed, verify with current sources or clearly label an estimate. Do not present a suggested reservation, purchase or booking as confirmed.

## Keep actions within authorization

Use available native tools or connected services only for what they support. Researching an option is not booking it; suggesting a reminder is not scheduling one. For recurring or future follow-up, use an actual supported scheduling mechanism before claiming it will run. Confirm authorization when spending money, booking, contacting others, sharing private details or taking other consequential actions.

For medical, legal or financial decisions, explain material uncertainty and avoid treating a general plan as professional diagnosis or advice. Minimize personal information and never infer sensitive preferences from unrelated context.

## Deliver

Provide an actionable result: recommended option and rationale, time-ordered plan, comparison or next step. Keep it concise for simple requests and more detailed when multiple constraints, risks or alternatives genuinely require it. Use [deep-research](../deep-research/SKILL.md) as the lead for evidence-heavy studies rather than duplicating a research process.
